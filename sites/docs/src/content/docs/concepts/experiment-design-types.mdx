Evidential offers two families of experiment design:

- **Frequentist (A/B testing)** keeps the split between arms fixed for the whole experiment. Use it to measure whether
  a change makes a difference, and by how much.
- **Bayesian (multi-armed bandits)** learns as results come in and sends more participants to the arms that are doing
  best. Use it to find and serve the best option while the experiment runs.

Within each family, the design you pick decides when participants are assigned, what Evidential needs from your data
warehouse, and how outcomes reach Evidential:

- **Pull:** Evidential reads outcomes from your data warehouse (on each snapshot or on a regular schedule).
- **Push:** Your app sends each participant's outcome to Evidential via the API.

Push and pull describe **outcomes only**. Your app always pulls assignments from Evidential (or downloads them as a CSV
for Preassigned A/B).

## At a Glance

| Design                             | Participants are assigned          | Data warehouse | Outcomes                    |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------- | --------------------------- |
| Preassigned A/B                    | All at once, before the start date | Required       | Pull (from your warehouse)  |
| Online A/B                         | One at a time, as they arrive      | Required       | Pull (from your warehouse)  |
| Multi-armed Bandit                 | One at a time, as they arrive      | Not needed     | Push (via API)              |
| Multi-armed Bandit (DWH-connected) | One at a time, as they arrive      | Required       | Pull (from your warehouse)  |
| Contextual Multi-armed Bandit      | One at a time, as they arrive      | Not needed     | Push (via API)              |

### Supported Data Sources

Evidential connects to **BigQuery**, **Redshift**, and **Postgres**. Bandits that don't use a data warehouse run on
your organization's **API Only** data source. If you use a source that isn't listed here yet,
[contact us](/contact/) to for support or [file a feature request](https://github.com/agency-fund/evidential-be/issues).

## Frequentist Designs

A frequentist experiment is a classic A/B test. You set the arms and how participants are split between them, then
compare the arms to see whether the change made a difference.

Every frequentist design:

- **Uses a participant table.** You point Evidential at a table or view in your data warehouse that lists your
  participants, one per row, with a column that uniquely identifies each one. Potential metrics, filters, and
  strata are also all drawn from columns in this table that describe each participant.
- **Pulls outcomes from your warehouse.** Evidential reads metric values for assigned participants directly from your
  data warehouse on each snapshot and compares each arm against the baseline arm. (Your app doesn't push outcomes to
  Evidential via the API.)

### Preassigned A/B

Evidential selects participants and assigns every one of them to an arm when you create the experiment.

- **Sample size.** Choose how many participants to include. Evidential draws them at random from the participants who
  match your filters.
- **Power check.** Before you launch, Evidential uses your data to estimate how many participants you need to detect
  the effect you care about, and shows how that changes with sample size.
- **Balanced arms.** You can pick strata, such as region or age group, so each arm gets a similar mix of participants.
- **Group assignment.** If your intervention reaches whole groups at once, such as a school or a village, you can
  assign groups instead of individuals. In the wizard, this is the optional **Cluster key**: choose the column that
  identifies each group and how many groups to include. Everyone who matches your filters in a selected group joins
  the same arm, and participants with no group are left out. Group assignment can't be combined with strata.
- **Getting assignments.** Download the full list as a CSV, fetch it through the API, or look up one participant at a
  time.

Choose Preassigned A/B when you know your participants ahead of time, or need the full list of who's in which arm
before the start date.

### Online A/B

Evidential assigns each participant the first time your app asks for their assignment, using the split you set.
There's no list of participants up front.

- **Your participant table is still required.** Evidential uses it to define your metrics and pull outcomes, but
  doesn't select participants from it.
- **No power check.** Online A/B doesn't include sample size planning.
- **Participants are assigned independently**, so there are no strata or group assignment.
- **Every request assigns.** Asking about a new participant always enrolls them. If you only want part of your traffic
  in the experiment, decide which participants to include in your own app first, then request assignments only for
  them.
- **Assignments don't change.** Once a participant has an arm, every later request returns the same arm.
- **The end date stops new assignments.** Participants who arrive after it aren't assigned an arm. Participants who
  already have one keep it.

Choose Online A/B when you don't know who will take part in advance, or participants arrive continuously.

### How Filters Work: Preassigned vs Online

Filters limit an experiment to the participants you want, for example *country is Kenya* or *signed up after
January 1*. Each filter compares one column to one or more values: the column **is one of** a list of values, **is not
one of** them, or, for numbers and dates, falls **between** two values. A participant must match all of an
experiment's filters.

The two frequentist designs apply the same filters in different ways.

**Preassigned A/B** applies filters to your participant table in the **power check** (so sample size estimates reflect
only matching participants) and when you **create the experiment** (to decide who can be selected). Eligibility is
decided once, from the data in your warehouse at that moment: people who match later aren't added, participants who
stop matching stay in the experiment, and changing filters requires a new experiment.

**Online A/B** can't look up a new participant in your warehouse when they arrive. Instead, your app sends the
participant's details along with the assignment request, and Evidential checks them against your filters:

- If the details match, the participant is assigned an arm. Once enrolled, they aren't removed if their details change.
- If they don't match, no arm is returned and the participant isn't enrolled.

To have filters applied, your app must use the assignment request that includes participant details (shown under
**Example API Calls** in the experiment's **Integration Guide**) and send a value for every filtered column—a missing
value counts as empty. A request without participant details assigns the participant regardless of your filters.

#### Limitations

- Filters can only use columns in the experiment's participant table.
- Filters always combine with "and". You can't create "this or that" conditions across different columns.
- Text matches are exact and case-sensitive. There's no partial or wildcard matching.
- **Between** works only with numbers and dates, and includes both end values. You can leave one end open, such as
  "on or after January 1".
- Empty values (NULL, not blank text) are handled explicitly. **Is one of** and **between** leave them out unless you
  add **OR NULL**. **Is not one of** keeps them by default; add **OR NULL** to exclude them too.
- Dates and times are in UTC.

## Bayesian Designs

Bayesian designs are multi-armed bandits. Each arm starts with an expected performance, called a prior. As results
come in, Evidential updates its estimate for each arm and sends more participants to the arms that look best.

Every bandit:

- **Has an outcome type**: **Binary** for yes/no results such as a click or a sign-up, or **Real-valued** for numbers
  such as a rating or time spent.
- **Starts from priors**, which you can set for each arm or have Evidential derive from starting weights.
- **Shows results as estimates** for each arm, which update as results arrive. There's no power check.
- **Stops new assignments at the end date.** Participants who arrive after it aren't assigned an arm; those who already
  have one keep it.

:::note[Use an A/B test to measure effects]
Bandits are built to find and serve the best option, not to measure the size of a difference. If you need a reliable
estimate of an effect, use an A/B test.
:::

### Multi-armed Bandit

The standard bandit. It doesn't need a data warehouse: your app requests an assignment for each participant and
pushes each participant's outcome back to Evidential via the API. To set it up, choose **Multi-armed Bandit** in the
experiment wizard, then **No data warehouse** on the data source step.

**Autofail** is available for this design. If a participant has no outcome after a time window you set, Evidential
records a default outcome you choose for them, such as a "no" for a binary outcome. Once recorded, the outcome can't be
replaced.

### Multi-armed Bandit (DWH-connected)

A Multi-armed Bandit that pulls outcomes from your data warehouse instead of having your app push them. In the
experiment wizard, choose **Multi-armed Bandit**, then on the data source step pick a data source, a table, the column
that identifies participants (**Unique ID**), and a **Target column** that holds each participant's outcome.

- **Your app requests assignments, but doesn't push outcomes.** Evidential pulls the target column on a regular
  schedule. The participant IDs your app uses must match the values in the column that identifies participants.
- **Outcomes can arrive late.** Empty values are checked again on later pulls, for about a week after the end date.
  Outcomes that are still empty after that stay empty.
- **The target column sets the outcome type.** A true/false column is a binary outcome. A numeric column is
  real-valued.
- **Autofail isn't available** for this design.

### Contextual Multi-armed Bandit

A contextual bandit learns which arm works best for different kinds of participants. Along with each assignment
request, your app sends context: information about the participant or their situation, such as their age or the time
of day.

- **At least one context is required.** Each context is either binary or real-valued.
- **Your app pushes each outcome** via the API, as with the standard Multi-armed Bandit. Autofail is available.
- **Results depend on context.** To see an arm's estimated performance, you choose context values.
- **No example calls.** The Integration Guide doesn't include example API calls for this design. See the
  [API reference](/api/) instead.

## Choosing a Design

| If you want to…                                                          | Use                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Assign a known list of participants before you start                     | Preassigned A/B                       |
| Assign whole groups, such as schools or villages                         | Preassigned A/B with group assignment |
| Measure the effect of a change on participants who arrive over time      | Online A/B                            |
| Find and serve the best option, with your app pushing outcomes via API   | Multi-armed Bandit                    |
| Find and serve the best option, with outcomes pulled from your warehouse | Multi-armed Bandit (DWH-connected)    |
| Find the best option for each kind of participant                        | Contextual Multi-armed Bandit         |
