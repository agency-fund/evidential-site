This page outlines the statistical methods and assumptions behind Evidential's experiment engine. For an overview of when
to use each experiment type, see [Experiment design types](/concepts/experiment-design-types/).

## Power Calculations

Before you launch a **Preassigned A/B** experiment (individual or cluster-randomized), Evidential runs a power check
against eligible participants in your participant table (after applying your experiment's filters). **Online A/B** and
**Bayesian (bandit)** designs don't include upfront power calculations.

- **Defaults and inputs:** Evidential derives baseline outcome statistics from eligible participants and uses an
  editable default of **80% power (1−β)** and **0.05 significance level (α)**. It computes both the **sample size
  needed** for your target effect size (specified as a percentage change from baseline) and an **MDE curve** showing
  the smallest effect you can detect across different sample sizes.
- **Numeric vs. binary metrics:** Numeric metrics use a **two-sample t-test**; binary metrics use a **two-sample normal
  approximation** (via Cohen's *h*).
- **Multi-arm and unbalanced splits:** When an experiment has more than two arms or custom arm weights, power is sized
  for the smallest treatment arm relative to the baseline arm so every arm meets your target power.
- **Cluster-randomized designs:** Sample size and MDE calculations account for clustering via the design effect
  (Eldridge et al., 2006; see also [this World Bank post](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/impactevaluations/different-sized-baskets-fruit-how-unequally-sized-clusters-can-lead-your-power)):

```text
DEFF = 1 + ICC × [(m − 1) + m × CV²]
```

where *m* is the average cluster size, *ICC* is the intracluster correlation coefficient, and *CV* is the coefficient
of variation in cluster sizes.

## Assignments

### Preassigned A/B

Static **stratified randomized assignment** across selected strata (with numeric strata bucketed into quartiles for
stratification), or simple random assignment when no strata are selected. Supports equal or custom arm weights.

### Preassigned A/B (Cluster-Randomized)

Static randomized assignment of whole **clusters**—such as schools, clinics, or villages—so everyone matching your
filters in a selected cluster joins the same arm. Clusters are stratified internally by cluster size so arms receive a
balanced mix of cluster sizes; custom strata aren't supported. Supports equal or custom arm weights at the cluster
level.

### Online A/B

Dynamic **simple random assignment** as participants arrive, without stratification or clustering. Higher risk of
imbalance in smaller samples.

### Bayesian (Bandits)

Dynamic **Thompson Sampling** (with optional top-two exploration) that shifts traffic toward better-performing arms as
outcomes arrive. See [Bayesian Designs](/concepts/experiment-design-types/#bayesian-designs) for details.

## Balance Checks

After randomization for **Preassigned A/B** experiments, Evidential checks whether arms are statistically balanced
across baseline characteristics whenever usable covariates exist—across your selected strata for individual
experiments, or across cluster size for cluster-randomized experiments (when cluster sizes vary).

- Evidential runs an **omnibus F-test of joint significance** from an OLS regression predicting treatment assignment
  (currently comparing the **first two arms**), using **HC1 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors** (or
  **cluster-robust standard errors** when clustering applies).
- Constant columns and unique non-numeric columns are excluded. Numeric columns with missing values are bucketed into
  quartiles, with missing values treated as their own category.
- Baseline characteristics should **not** predict treatment assignment; a high p-value indicates balanced arms.

## Strata

Strata (also known as **covariates** or **controls**) are pre-treatment participant-level
characteristics drawn from columns in your participant table. They are available for individual
**Preassigned A/B** experiments (not cluster-randomized or Online A/B), and are used to:

- Stratify treatment assignment so each arm receives a balanced mix of participants, and
- Verify baseline balance in post-assignment balance checks.

Each participant's strata values (as recorded at assignment time) are exported alongside assignments so you can run
post-hoc subgroup or covariate-adjusted analyses yourself. Evidential's built-in analysis regression does not currently
include strata as covariates (see [Frequentist Analysis: Regression](#frequentist-analysis-regression) below).

## Data Warehouse Metrics

Outcome metrics for frequentist experiments live in your data warehouse table at the **participant level**.

These should be the **primary metrics** your organization holds itself accountable to. They should
ideally match the operational and impact metrics you already use to evaluate your programs.

## Frequentist Analysis: Regression

For frequentist experiments, Evidential estimates the **intent-to-treat average treatment effect (ATE)** for each
metric using **linear OLS with arm indicators** (no covariates):

```text
Metricᵢ = Σⱼ βⱼ × Armᵢⱼ + εᵢ
```

- Each arm's estimated treatment effect is the contrast against the baseline arm (`β_treatment − β_baseline`),
  interpreted as the intent-to-treat average treatment effect on the chosen metric.
- Inference uses **HC1 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors** for individually randomized experiments and
  **cluster-robust standard errors** (clustered by your cluster key) for cluster-randomized experiments.

:::note[Coming soon: covariate-adjusted regression]
We plan to add covariate-adjusted estimation:

```text
Metricᵢ = β₀ + β₁ × Treatmentᵢ + Controlsᵢ + εᵢ
```

where `Controls` include pre-specified strata as well as pre-treatment values for the outcome
metric, to reduce standard errors and therefore improve precision.
:::

## Bayesian (Bandit) Analysis

For multi-armed and contextual bandits, Evidential updates each arm's posterior distribution as outcomes arrive
(Beta-Binomial for binary outcomes, Normal models for real-valued outcomes, and Bayesian regression for contextual
bandits) and reports posterior estimates and credible intervals rather than frequentist p-values.

*Evidential handles all the above automatically — so you can focus on learning what works, not on the math behind it.*
