Evidential doesn't manage passwords. People sign in with the account they already use at work, such as Google, Okta, or
another single sign-on service. That service, called the **identity provider**, confirms who someone is. Password rules,
two-step verification, and closing accounts when someone leaves stay with your organization's existing IT setup.

Signing in through the identity provider doesn't by itself give someone access. Evidential keeps its own record of who
has an account and what they can see.

## Who Gets an Account

By default, only people who have been invited can sign in. You invite someone by adding their email address as a member
of an organization, or an administrator can add them from **Manage Users**. Their account is created then, and they can
sign in with that email address.

An administrator can widen this under **Sign-Up Policy** on the **Evidential Settings** page:

- **Invited users only**: the default.
- **Invited users and users in the configured domains**: anyone whose email address is at a domain listed under
    **Domain Settings** with **Allow sign-up** turned on, such as `example.org`, gets an account the first time they
    sign in. Domains must match exactly, so allowing `example.org` doesn't allow `research.example.org`.
- **Anyone**: anyone who can sign in through the identity provider gets an account.

People who sign up on their own (without an invite) start without any organization. They're asked to create one, or a
member can add them to an existing one.

The first person to sign in to a new Evidential installation becomes its first administrator.

## What Members Can See and Do

Data sources, experiments, API keys, and webhooks all belong to an organization. Members see the organizations they
belong to and nothing else.

Within an organization, every regular member has the same access. Any member can add other members or remove them. There
are no finer-grained roles, such as read-only members.

## Administrators

Administrators (labeled **Privileged** in the app) have installation-wide access. Keep this list small. Administrators
can:

- See every organization on the installation and manage its members, webhooks, and settings, even if they aren't a
    member of it. To work with an organization's data sources or experiments, they add themselves as a member first.
- Manage users from the **Manage Users** page: invite users, add or remove them from organizations, grant or remove
    administrator access, and delete accounts.
- Change the sign-up policy and domain settings.

Evidential won't let you remove or delete the last administrator, so your team can't be locked out.

## Sessions

Sessions last up to 12 hours, after which you'll need to sign in again. Signing out ends your sessions on every device,
not just the current one.

## Access for Your Applications

Your applications don't sign in as a person. Software that assigns participants or reports outcomes uses an
[API key](/guides/integrate-with-the-api/), which belongs to one data source rather than to a person. Deleting a member's
account doesn't revoke API keys they created, so delete those keys separately when you no longer need them.
